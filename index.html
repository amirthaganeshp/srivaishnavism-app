Srivaishnavism

A journey into the heart of divine love, surrender, and the glorious tradition of the Alwars and Ramanujacharya.

What is Srivaishnavism?

Srivaishnavism is a vibrant and ancient Hindu tradition deeply rooted in the Vedas, Upanishads, and the soulful Tamil hymns of the Alwars (the Divya Prabandham). Central to this tradition is the loving surrender to Lord Narayana (Vishnu) and His divine consort, Sri (Lakshmi), who acts as the compassionate mediator between the soul and the Lord.

Guided by illustrious Acharyas like Sri Bhagavad Ramanuja, Srivaishnavism teaches Visishtadvaita—the philosophy that the supreme Lord is the soul of both the sentient beings and the insentient universe, holding everything in a harmonious, divine relationship.

The 12 Alwars

Sri Ramanujacharya

Sriman Nathamuni

The first Acharya in the historical lineage. He revived the Nalayira Divya Prabandham which was lost over time, learning them directly from Nammalwar in a yogic trance. He set the hymns to music and introduced them into temple worship.

Yamunacharya (Alavandar)

The grandson of Nathamuni. He established the philosophical foundations of Sri Vaishnavism, proving the supremacy of Lord Narayana and the path of Bhakti. His renowned works include Stotra Ratnam and Siddhi Trayam.

Sri Ramanujacharya

The most prominent Acharya and the chief proponent of Visishtadvaita Vedanta. He systematized the philosophy, established extensive temple administration protocols (especially at Srirangam), and opened the path of salvation (Saranagati) to all, irrespective of societal barriers.

Swami Vedanta Desikan

A towering polymath of the post-Ramanuja era. He defended Visishtadvaita through prolific writings in Sanskrit, Tamil, and Manipravalam. Known as the 'Lion among poets and logicians' (Kavitarkika Simha).

Manavala Mamunigal

Considered a reincarnation of Ramanuja. He spent his life writing extensive, profound commentaries on the Divya Prabandham and the works of earlier Acharyas, making the esoteric knowledge accessible to everyone.

Nalayira Divya Prabandham

The Nalayira Divya Prabandham consists of 4000 verses sung by the 12 Alwars. Here is a detailed breakdown of the major compositions, their authors, and significance.

Composition Author (Alwar) Verses Context & Significance
Mudal Ayiram (First Thousand - 947 Verses)
Tirupallandu Periyalwar 12 Sung to ward off the evil eye from the Lord when He appeared on Garuda; the foundational prayer.
Periyalwar Tirumozhi Periyalwar 461 Details the childhood pastimes of Lord Krishna with deep maternal affection.
Tiruppavai Andal 30 Sung during Margazhi month, waking up the Lord. Considered the essence of all Upanishads.
Nachiyar Tirumozhi Andal 143 Expresses intense bridal mysticism and longing to marry Lord Ranganatha.
Perumal Tirumozhi Kulasekhara Alwar 105 Expresses profound devotion to Lord Rama and the desire to be anything on Tirumala hills.
Tiruchanda Virutham Thirumazhisai Alwar 120 Composed in a rhythmic meter, establishing Lord Narayana's supremacy.
Tirumalai Thondaradippodi Alwar 45 A garland of verses emphasizing that the Lord's name is sweeter than Vaikuntham itself.
Tiruppalliyezhuchi Thondaradippodi Alwar 10 The beautiful morning hymn to wake up Lord Ranganatha at Srirangam.
Amalanadipiran Thiruppaan Alwar 10 A passionate, top-to-bottom description of Lord Ranganatha's beauty leading to merging into Him.
Kanninun Siruthambu Madhurakavi Alwar 11 Expresses ultimate Acharya Bhakti, stating that Nammalwar is greater than the Lord Himself.
Periya Thirumozhi (Second Thousand - 1134 Verses)
Periya Tirumozhi Thirumangai Alwar 1084 Extensive hymns covering numerous Divya Desams, filled with majestic language and profound devotion.
Tirukkurunthandagam Thirumangai Alwar 20 Short verses deeply analyzing the nature of the soul and the Lord.
Tirunedunthandagam Thirumangai Alwar 30 Highly philosophical verses using the long verse meter.
Iyarpa (Third Thousand - 817 Verses)
Mudal Tiruvandhadhi Poigai Alwar 100 The first light of devotion, using the earth as a vessel to see the Lord at Thirukkovilur.
Irandam Tiruvandhadhi Bhoothath Alwar 100 The second light of devotion, using love as the vessel.
Moondram Tiruvandhadhi Pey Alwar 100 The ecstatic vision of the Lord and Goddess Lakshmi holding divine weapons.
Nanmugan Tiruvandhadhi Thirumazhisai Alwar 96 Fiercely establishes the supremacy of Lord Narayana over all other deities including Brahma (Nanmugan).
Tiruviruttam Nammalwar 100 The essence of Rig Veda, appealing to the Lord to end the cycle of samsara.
Tiruvasiriyam Nammalwar 7 The essence of Yajur Veda.
Periya Tiruvandhadhi Nammalwar 87 The essence of Atharva Veda.
Tiruvezhukootrirukkai Thirumangai Alwar 1 A unique composition structured mathematically like a temple chariot.
Siriya Tirumadal Thirumangai Alwar 40 (lines) Expresses intense Nayaki bhava (bridal mysticism).
Periya Tirumadal Thirumangai Alwar 78 (lines) Further expressions of intense longing and bridal mysticism.
Thiruvaymoli (Fourth Thousand - 1102 Verses)
Thiruvaymoli Nammalwar 1102 The crown jewel of the Prabandham, celebrated as the Tamil Sama Veda. It captures the entire philosophy of Visishtadvaita and the soul's yearning for God.

Prapatthi (Saranagati)

The crown jewel of Srivaishnavism is the doctrine of Prapatthi or absolute self-surrender to the Lord. Recognizing one's own inability to cross the ocean of samsara (the cycle of birth and death) through karma, jnana, or bhakti alone, the soul casts its entire spiritual burden at the lotus feet of the Lord in supreme faith.

Saranagati is marked by six distinct qualities: the intent to do what pleases the Lord, avoiding what displeases Him, unwavering faith in His protection, a plea for Him to be the savior, a sense of total helplessness, and the final offering of one's self (Atma Nivedanam).

The 108 Divya Desams

Divya Desams are the 108 Vishnu temples mentioned in the works of the Alwars. These are primarily located in India, with one in Nepal, and two existing in the celestial realms.
